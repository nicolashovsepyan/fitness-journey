# The method — who we follow, and what we take from each

Source: `Fitness journey - youtube channels for fundementals .xlsx`, in this
folder. That spreadsheet is the authority; this file is what I do with it.

**I cannot watch video.** That needs saying plainly, because the spreadsheet
asks me to. I can read a playlist and pull its titles, lengths and links —
that is how `spine/followalongs.json` was built — but I cannot watch a clip
and extract a progression from it. What I *can* do is apply what these
coaches actually teach, which I know, and name the ones I am unsure about
rather than inventing a rep scheme and attributing it to somebody.

## The roster, and what each one changes

| Coach | What we take |
|---|---|
| **FitnessFAQs** (Daniel Vadnal) | The spine of the upper body. Calisthenics stops progressing when you stop adding load — so weighted dips and weighted chins carry the heavy work, and the barbell keeps only the deadlift and the squat. |
| **Paul Twyman** | Handstand. Wall-facing holds and shape work long before freestanding, which is why the program holds chest-to-wall rather than chasing a free balance. |
| **Bodyweight Warrior** (Tom Merrick) | Handstand practice and the mobility that makes it possible. Overlaps Twyman; used as the second opinion. |
| **Knees Over Toes Guy** (Ben Patrick) | The knee gets robust by being loaded through its full range, not by avoiding it. Tibialis and KOT calf before every lower session; Nordic and reverse Nordic inside them. Also the reason jumping is *earned*, not assumed. |
| **Squat University** (Aaron Horschig) | Ankles before knees, hips before bar. Squat and hinge mechanics, and what to do when something hurts rather than stopping. |
| **Jeff Nippard** | Volume, frequency and exercise selection by stimulus-to-fatigue. The reason every pattern is trained twice a week and the reason the work blocks are antagonist pairs. |
| **Ryan Humiston** | Leg protocols. Tension and time under it over pure load — the third block on both lower days is his flavour rather than a strength flavour. |
| **Pavel Tsatsouline** | Kettlebell. Heavy, snappy, never to fatigue. Swings are power, not conditioning. |
| **Strength Side** | Mobility and animal movement. The 22 follow-alongs come from here. |
| **Calisthenics Movement** | General calisthenics reference for progressions and standards. |
| **Saturno Movement** | Calisthenics meets yoga and mobility. |
| **AthleanX** (Jeff Cavaliere) | Joint-friendly selection and form faults. The corrective lens over everything else. |
| **Tim Ferriss** | Minimum effective dose. The reason a finisher is capped at five minutes. |

## The one real tension, and how it is resolved

You like jumping — the spreadsheet says so under Knees Over Toes — and you
have a knee that has needed working around. Those are not in conflict, they
are in *order*. Patrick's own sequence earns plyometrics at the far end of a
ladder that starts with tibialis raises and ATG split squats.

So block one has no jumping and no landing anywhere in it. If the knee stays
quiet through four weeks, block two starts the jumping at the bottom of that
ladder. That is written into the program's week-4 rule.

## The follow-alongs

22 videos from Strength Side, in `spine/followalongs.json`, regenerated by
`node tools/build-followalongs.mjs --fetch`.

17 of them run 12 minutes or less. They are tagged by area — hips 6, squat 6,
full body 6, flow 5, legs 2, shoulders 1, breath 1.

They are deliberately **not** in the movement library and never will be. The
spreadsheet was explicit: *"this is more mobility and flexibility, so it
should not come up if we are in a working session."* A follow-along is not an
exercise, it is ten minutes of somebody talking you through a flow, and the
only thing the app has to do is play it.
