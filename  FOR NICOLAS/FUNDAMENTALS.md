# The fundamentals — 15 / 30 / 50

Built from your archive, not invented: the YouTube fundamentals sheet,
`YOUTUBE_LIBRARY.md`, `TRAINING_ARCHIVE.md`, and the tier endpoints already in
`_build_push_curation.js` and `_build_core_curation.js`.

**Functional first.** Almost everything here is bodyweight or a single
implement. The barbell appears three times in fifty, and only at the top.

---

## First — does 15 / 30 / 50 make sense?

**Yes, but one word needs changing, and it matters.**

Fundamentals do not get replaced, they get added to. A beginner's push-up is
the same push-up an advanced athlete does in their warm-up ten years later.
So the lists have to be cumulative: **advanced = all 50, including all 15.**
That is exactly what you said and it is right.

The word to change is *"do."* Nobody trains 50 movements a week. What the
numbers actually mean:

> **15 / 30 / 50 is what you have MASTERED. Roughly 12–15 is what you TRAIN
> in any given week, at any level.**

The advanced athlete is not doing more work than the beginner. They are
choosing their twelve from a deck of fifty instead of a deck of fifteen. That
is what more advanced *means*, and it is also why their programme can be
varied without ever becoming random.

Worth saying out loud in the app, because it kills the two things that make
people quit: *I am not doing enough* and *I have to keep finding new
exercises.*

## The families

Your twelve slots, plus the two you flagged as neglected. **Fourteen
families.** Every one of the fifty belongs to exactly one.

Squat · Hinge · Single leg · Horizontal push · Vertical push · Horizontal
pull · Vertical pull · Explosive · Carry/grip · Full body · Core static ·
Core dynamic · **Mobility** · **Aerobic**

Mobility and aerobic are not warm-ups and cool-downs here. They are families
with progressions of their own, exactly like the squat.

# BEGINNER — 15

One per family, plus one. Every single one of these stays in the programme
forever. Nothing on this list ever gets "outgrown."

| # | Movement | Family | Why it is non-negotiable |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Bodyweight squat** | Squat | Sit down and stand up. If this goes, independence goes. |
| 2 | **Hip hinge (bodyweight RDL)** | Hinge | The single most protective pattern for a back. Taught before it is loaded. |
| 3 | **Reverse lunge** | Single leg | Kinder on the knee than a forward lunge, and it finds the weak side immediately. |
| 4 | **Push-up** | Horizontal push | Incline it as far as needed. Still the same movement. |
| 5 | **Pike push-up** | Vertical push | Your note: fundamental, and the whole handstand line starts here. Elevate the hips to regress. |
| 6 | **Inverted row** | Horizontal pull | Under a bar or a table. The direct counter to sitting. |
| 7 | **Dead hang → negative pull-up** | Vertical pull | The entry to the hardest family. Hanging is training even before a rep exists. |
| 8 | **Farmer carry** | Carry / grip | Grip strength predicts more than any other single number. Two bags of shopping. |
| 9 | **Jump squat (land soft)** | Explosive | Power is the first quality lost with age. The landing is the fundamental, not the jump. |
| 10 | **Burpee** | Full body | Get to the floor, get back up. Conditioning and the floor-transfer in one. |
| 11 | **Plank** | Core static | Hold a line under load. |
| 12 | **Dead bug** | Core dynamic | First rung of your own core chain: dead bug → hollow rock → hollow hold → L-sit. |
| 13 | **Joint prep — CARs circuit** | Mobility | Shoulder CARs, wrist circles, hip 90/90. `YOUTUBE_LIBRARY.md`: *non-negotiable before any upper body or skill session.* |
| 14 | **Deep squat hold** | Mobility | The most useful position a human can own. Ankle, hip and spine in one. |
| 15 | **Zone 2 — 20–30 min walk or easy jog** | Aerobic | Galpin's base. The adaptation almost nobody builds and everybody needs. |

## The three things this changes

### 1. The ladder needs a second axis — level, not just equipment

Your note: *"pike push-up is the right one but should we have handstand PU for
more advanced?"*

Yes, and the current design cannot express that. It picks a movement from
equipment alone. It has to pick from **equipment × level**:

| Vertical push | Beginner | Intermediate | Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nothing but a floor | Pike push-up | Wall handstand hold | Handstand push-up |
| Barbell | Overhead press | Overhead press | Push press / jerk |

Same for every family. This is a real change to how the deck resolves, and it
is the right one — a bare-floor advanced athlete should be asked about a
handstand push-up, not a pike push-up.

### 2. Let them choose push-up or bench press

Your note: *"I would personally pick push-up over bench press, but let the
user decide."* Agreed, and it is one small control: where two rungs are close
in signal, the card shows the higher one with a quiet **"I'd rather answer
about the push-up"** underneath. Default stays the higher rung, so nothing is
lost for people who do not care.

I would only offer it where it is genuinely a preference, not a regression:
bench press ↔ push-up, back squat ↔ bodyweight squat, deadlift ↔ single-leg
RDL. Not pull-up ↔ table row — that one is a real difference in capability
and letting people opt down would corrupt the assessment.

### 3. Mobility and aerobic are families, so they need session rules

They cannot just sit in a list. From your own sheet:

> *"they are all for mobility, they are all 10 minutes approx which is great
> as we can have it as part of a session… this should not come up if we are
> in a working session (hyper, conditioning, strength)"*

So the rule is already yours: **mobility follow-alongs are their own session
type, never a block inside a strength or conditioning session.** What *does*
belong inside a working session is the joint prep (#13) — sixty to ninety
seconds, and only for the joints that session is about.
