# What the survey is missing for a Galpin-grade program

You asked whether we are collecting enough to build the program properly.
**We are not, and your own coach console already says so** — it has three
places where it writes *"Not asked"* against a Galpin step it is trying to
work through.

Below: what is missing, what to cut to pay for it, and what I would do.

---

## The three gaps the console flags itself

### 1. Sleep — Galpin step 9, recovery

The console's own words: *"Sleep was never asked in onboarding. For a
45-year-old whose weight creeps up fast, that is a real gap."*

Without a wearable there is no HRV, so Galpin's primary objective recovery
marker is unavailable. Sleep is the one input that replaces it. It is also the
input that decides whether someone can take 4 sessions a week or 3 — under six
hours and the volume ceiling drops whatever the person says they want.

**One question. One tap.**

### 2. How much room training actually gets — Galpin step 4

Galpin's ten-point quadrant: split ten points across business, relationships,
fitness and recovery. He treats a program that ignores this as *already
failed*. The console records this as *"Not asked — inferred from 45, kids,
life gets busy."* Inferring it is guessing.

I would not ask anyone to split ten points across four areas — it is abstract
and people answer it aspirationally. Ask the outcome instead: how much room
does training realistically get right now.

**One question. One tap.**

### 3. Aerobic base — Galpin step 1, the untested adaptations

Galpin's framework has nine adaptations. Our twelve cards measure strength,
muscular endurance and a bit of power. **Nothing measures aerobic capacity at
all** — and that is two of the nine (max aerobic, long-duration endurance),
plus it is the base he says almost nobody builds.

The console's line: *an unmeasured adaptation should be recorded as unknown,
not assumed.* Right now we assume.

**One question. One tap.** Not a test — a self-placement, same as the sliders.

---

## What I would cut to pay for it

The survey is long enough. These three earn their place; three others do not.

| Cut | Why |
|---|---|
| **"Which numbers do you look at?"** (tracker metrics) | A whole ranked screen. Interesting, but it changes nothing about what gets programmed. |
| **"What does your weight do?"** | Already answered twice — by the 90-day focus (lean / muscle) and the body-shape they scrolled to. |
| **Merge the two "where are you going" screens** | The long horizon and the 90-day focus are the same question at two ranges. The horizon is largely inferable from age plus focus. One screen, not two. |

**Net: +3 questions, −3 screens.** The survey does not get longer, and the
program gets three inputs it currently guesses at.

---

## Where they go

No new chapter. The structure already fits.

| Chapter | Change |
|---|---|
| 2 · Where you're going | Two screens merge into one |
| 3 · Where you are now | Drop weight-tendency and tracker-metrics |
| 4 · Your training | **Add aerobic base** — right after the twelve cards, it completes the assessment |
| 5 · Your week | **Add sleep** and **add training-room** — they sit with days, length and other-activities, which is where capacity is already being worked out |

That last chapter becomes the honest one: *this is what your week can actually
hold.* Days, session length, other sport, sleep, and how much room training
gets. Galpin's steps 4, 5 and 9 in one place.

## Two more I considered and rejected

**Training age** ("how many years have you trained seriously?") — genuinely
useful; a six-month lifter and a ten-year lifter with the same push-up number
need different progressions. But the slider answers plus "how active are you
right now" already separate them most of the time, and I would rather not add
a fourth question. **Worth adding if you disagree — it is the strongest of the
rejected ones.**

**Nutrition direction** (deficit / maintenance / surplus) — matters for
hypertrophy versus fat loss, but the 90-day focus already says which one they
want, and asking about food at intake invites a conversation the survey cannot
have. Better at the first check-in.
