Quick Timer — home screen icon

The chronograph.

Every part of the timer mark, live, at the sizes the phone actually draws. It opens on exactly what ships today, read back out of the SVG rather than remembered: hand at 21, register at 00, two marks cleared either side of each number, the sunray face, jade crown.

The number worth watching here is clear — how much graduation comes off around each numeral. At 0 the tick lines sit directly behind the digits and the 30 turns to mush, because at six o'clock the numerals run sideways and are about three times wider across the dial than 15 and 45 are. The lab works this out from each numeral's real footprint at its own angle, so it stays right when you change the size.

The dial face

All of these are meant to be felt rather than seen. Rim is the only one that does something structural — darkening just the outer edge makes the ring read as standing above the face instead of drawn on it — and it is the only one still doing anything at 60px.

The ring

The opening is the gap at the top the crown stands in. Close it and the crown sits on the tube instead of in it.

The graduation

Clear is how many marks come off each side of a number. One tick is six degrees. At 0 the lines sit behind the digits.

The numbers

15, 30 and 45 stand where their tick lines were, out in the graduation band, which is what leaves the middle of the dial to the hand and the register. 60 is missing because the crown is standing on it.

The register

It sits at 12 o'clock, in the window the crown gap already leaves open. That is what pushed the hand into the lower half, and it is why nothing crowds the middle any more.

The hand

The case

The crown is the one piece that can go either way. Jade ties the top of the mark to the hand and the register; pink makes it part of the case and quietens the top. The icon ships jade.
