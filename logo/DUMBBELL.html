Fitness Journey — the weight gauge

Plates on the floor.

The dumbbell is a 3D object lying in the ground plane, drawn in the same one-point projection as the dashboard's sunset floor and yawed around the vertical — which is what turns the plates from strokes into discs. And the weight is counted, not scaled: 20s, 10s and 5s, loaded the way a bar actually gets loaded. Sixty is three big plates. Seventy-five is three big, a ten and a five. The pair stands on one level with a mirror line between them, and every plate colour is yours — dark is heavy, so the denominations read by value as well as by size.

Width and height are separate multipliers, both 100% at the artwork's own proportion. The shadow and the number follow them, so the bell stays planted and the weight stays on its medallion however you stretch it.

Growth is how fast the bell gets bigger with weight. 0.333 is cube root — mass to length, the same law the plates use. A real rack runs a little under that, because the number on the label includes a handle whose weight barely changes.

All four are wired to one source. Everything on this page is drawn by logo/equipment.mjs, which is injected into every screen that shows a gauge by tools/build-equipment.mjs — so a change you make here reaches the app instead of stopping at this page, the same way the logo works. Run --check to prove they still agree.

The kettlebell is the one exception, and it is honest about it. It is a rendered image rather than geometry: four drawn ones were built and none of them held up, so the drawing was dropped rather than shipped half-right. That is why it has no yaw — a photograph cannot be turned — and why its handle cannot be coloured apart from its bell. The weight on the medallion IS live text, so it is right at every step of the range.
