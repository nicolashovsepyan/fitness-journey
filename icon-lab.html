Your knobs.

Two icons in one panel — the filled tile and the triangle — each with every colour and size live, at the scales the phone actually draws.

Each has one number worth watching. On the filled tile it is contrast: jade on pink runs about 1.4:1, and under roughly 2:1 the dumbbell stops having an edge and becomes a shape you infer from its shadow. On the triangle it is clearance — the gap between the plates and the sloping edge. Under 8px the real build refuses to write the icon at all, so that number is not advice.

The tube controls apply to both. A lit core is what separates neon from a line drawing of neon, and it is now a toggle rather than a fixed choice — along with core colour and width, the inner bloom, and two plates instead of three. Everything saved before this keeps its flat stroke; the core is off until you ask for it.

The dumbbell

Shadow starts at 0, which is the icon as it ships today. On a dark tile it does little; it earns its keep the moment you lighten the ground.

The triangle narrows as it drops, so the tightest point is the bottom corner of the smallest plate against the sloping edge — not the bar. Height is what buys room, not size.

The tube

Cut handle breaks the bar in the middle, the same dead run the triangle has. Angle tips it off the horizontal — the clearance number follows the rotation, so it stays honest.

A real tube blows out to near-white at the centre and keeps its colour in the halo. Drawing one flat stroke is a picture of neon; the core is the thing that makes it neon. Outline glow only exists on the filled tile — the triangle has its own Glow already.

Two plates instead of three: six vertical strokes across 40px of icon merge into a smear, two heavier ones still read as a dumbbell. It changes the clearance number, so check it.

A bloom needs darkness to bloom into. On a light tile the core still works and the glow does almost nothing — that is the medium, not a bug.

The signature

Your drawing, not a redraw of it. The export also came back as an SVG and it did not match — overlaid on the artwork it missed the f's loop and the swash. The raster is the real thing, so the raster is what ships.

Line weight genuinely re-weights it: the alpha is dilated or eroded, so the stroke thickens and thins along its own curves. Core eats the shape in from its edges to light the centre, the same structure as the tube.

The swash and the dot are part of the one drawing, so they cannot be switched off separately.

The wall

Running bond, two courses to the repeat so it does not read as a grid, and four tones across the bricks because real brick is never one colour. Brick replaces the tile fill, so its lift, drop and glow stop applying.
